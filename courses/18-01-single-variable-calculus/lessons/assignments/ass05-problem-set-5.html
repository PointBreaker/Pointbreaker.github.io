Problem Set 5：面积与体积

温度冷却模型、 Fresnel 积分 \(\int\cos(t^2)dt\) 的图像分析、以及一个由不等式 \(\frac{z^2}{2}

类型：Problem Set 5（Part I + Part II） 截止：2006-10-27 13:55 覆盖：Lecture 21, 22（Part I 含 Lecture 19–20）

学习目标

  • 用分离变量解指数增长/冷却模型，用定积分表示「冷却度日数 (cooling degree days)」。
  • 分析无法用初等函数表达的积分 \(\int\cos(t^2)dt\)（Fresnel 积分），用 FTC 判断极值、拐点、奇偶性。

  • 通过换元把反常积分与 Fresnel 积分相互转化。

主题梳理与示例

1. 温度模型与冷却度日数（Part II/1）

Boston 温度满足 \(y'(t)=y(t)/100\)，初值 \(y(0)=65\)°F。分离变量：

\[\frac{dy}{y}=\frac{dt}{100}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \ln y=\frac{t}{100}+C\]

由 \(y(0)=65\) 得 \(y(t)=65e^{t/100}\)。连续平均值定义：\(\frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b y\,dt\)。冷却度日数 = 每日平均温度减去 65 的总和，用面积表示为：

\[\text{CDD}=65\cdot 50-\int_0^{50}y(t)\,dt=3250-\int_0^{50}65e^{t/100}\,dt\]

第 (c) 小题（额外学分）要求用黎曼和直接算这个积分，与 PS4 的 \(e^x\) 黎曼和做法类似，再次凸显 FTC 的威力。

2. Fresnel 积分 \(\displaystyle f(x)=\int_0^x\cos(t^2)\,dt\)（Part II/2）

这是无法用初等函数表达的积分。关键问题：

  • 奇偶性：\(\cos(t^2)\) 是偶函数，所以 \(f(x)\) 是奇函数。
  • 临界点：由 FTC2，\(f'(x)=\cos(x^2)\)，零点在 \(x^2=\pi/2+k\pi\)，即 \(x=\pm\sqrt{\pi/2},\,\pm\sqrt{3\pi/2},\dots\)。
  • 极值/拐点：\(f''(x)=-2x\sin(x^2)\)，符号变化给出拐点位置。
  • 估值：\(f(0.1)\approx\int_0^{0.1}(1-t^2/2+t^4/24-\cdots)\,dt\approx 0.1-0.001/6\)。

第 (e) 小题用换元把 \(f\) 与标准 Fresnel 积分 \(g(x)=\int_0^x\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}u^2)\,du\) 以及反常积分 \(\int_0^x\frac{\cos v}{\sqrt{v}}\,dv\) 联系起来：

\[f(x)=c_1\,g(c_2x),\qquad f(x)=c\,h(x^2)\]

Fresnel 积分是「积分存在但原函数非初等」的典型例子，在光学（衍射）中有重要应用。

3. 不等式区域体积（Part II/3）

区域 \(x>0,y>0,z>0\)，满足 \(\frac{z^2}{2}0\)、\(y>0\)、\(\frac{z^2}{2} \[A(z)=\frac12 z^2-\frac12\left(\frac{z^2}{2}\right)^2\quad\text{（需确认边界交点）}\]

体积 \(V=\int_0^2 A(z)\,dz\)（\(z\) 的范围由 \(x+yz^2/2\) 的相容性决定）。

常见坑

  • 冷却度日数符号：是「平均温度 - 65」的总和，65 减去积分对应面积差。
  • Fresnel 积分奇偶性：被积函数偶 → 积分奇，不要搞反。
  • 体积方法选择：绕 \(y\) 轴旋转时，若函数是 \(y=f(x)\)，通常柱壳（\(dx\)）更方便；绕 \(x\) 轴则圆盘（\(dy\)）。
  • 反常积分收敛：\(\int_0^1\frac{\cos v}{\sqrt{v}}\,dv\) 在 0 处被积函数无界，但积分收敛（比较 \(1/\sqrt{v}\)）。

自测小结

指数模型

\(y'=ky\) → \(y=y_0e^{kt}\)，冷却度日数用面积差表示。

Fresnel 积分

非初等原函数，用 FTC 分析极值/拐点/奇偶。

体积方法

圆盘 vs. 柱壳：由旋转轴与函数形式决定。

反常积分

\(\int\frac{\cos v}{\sqrt{v}}\,dv\) 在 0 无界但收敛。

Problem 1 Temperature model and cooling degree days First fundamental theorem of calculus · Differential equations · Average value of a function · Area between curves · Riemann sums

Suppose that at the beginning of day 0, some time last summer, the temperature in Boston was y(0)=65°F and that over a 50-day period, the temperature increased according to the rule y'(t)=y(t)/100, with time t measured in days.

  1. (3 pts) Find the formula for y. Draw a graph of temperature on days 3 and 4 (3 ≤ t ≤ 5), labeling with the correct day and shading the regions whose areas represent the average temperature each of the two days.
  2. (3 pts) The number of cooling degree days is the sum for each day of the difference between the average temperature for that day and 65°. Draw a second graph of y for the whole 50 days and shade the region whose area represents the total number of degree days. Write a formula for this total area as the difference between 65·50 and a definite integral. Evaluate the definite integral using the fundamental theorem of calculus. (Alternatively, write the whole quantity as an integral expressing the area between curves.)
  3. (+5 extra credit) Compute the definite integral in part (b) directly by evaluating a lower Riemann sum and taking a limit. Follow the procedure in Problem 5, PS4, but with different scale factors.

公式与已知条件

  • y(0) = 65°F
  • y'(t) = y(t)/100
  • Continuous average of f on [a,b]: (1/(b-a)) ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx; here b-a=1 so average equals the integral
  • Cooling degree days = Σ (average temperature - 65) over days
解题入口

Solve the differential equation for y. Use the fundamental theorem of calculus to evaluate the definite integral. Alternatively express as area between curves (Lecture 21, 7.2).

完成后检查

  • Verify y(0)=65
  • Verify y'(t)=y(t)/100 for the found formula
  • Check the definite integral evaluation via FTC

Problem 2 Fresnel integral Fresnel integrals · Critical points · Local extrema · Inflection points · Change of variables · Even and odd functions · Improper integrals

Consider the function f(x) = ∫₀ˣ cos(t²) dt. There is no expression for f(x) in terms of standard elementary functions. It is known as a Fresnel integral, along with the corresponding sine integral.

  1. (2 pts) Draw a rough sketch of cos(t²), showing the first positive and negative zeros. What does the curve look like at t=0? Is the function even or odd?
  2. (2 pts) List the critical points of f(x) in the entire range -∞ < x < ∞. Which critical points are local maxima and which ones are local minima?
  3. (4 pts) Sketch the graph of f on the interval -2 ≤ x ≤ 2, with labels for the critical points and inflection points. (The drawing should be qualitatively correct, but just estimate the values of f at the labelled points.)
  4. (2 pts) Estimate f(0.1) to six decimal places.
  5. (6 pts total) Fresnel integrals are sometimes expressed using different scaling of the variables. (i) Let g(x) = ∫₀ˣ cos((π/2)u²) du. Make a change of variables to show that f(x) = c1·g(c2·x) for some constants c1 and c2. Why did we choose the factor π/2? (ii) Let h(x) = ∫₀ˣ (cos v)/√v dv. (Improper integral; 1/√v is infinite at v=0.) Make a different change of variable to show that f(x) = c·h(x²) for some constant c (assume x>0). (iii) Let k(x) = √x ∫₀¹ cos(xt²) dt, x>0. Use the change of variable z = xt² and part (ii) to find the relationship between the functions k and f.

公式与已知条件

  • f(x) = ∫₀ˣ cos(t²) dt
  • g(x) = ∫₀ˣ cos((π/2)u²) du
  • h(x) = ∫₀ˣ (cos v)/√v dv (improper; 1/√v infinite at v=0)
  • k(x) = √x ∫₀¹ cos(xt²) dt, x>0
  • h is continuous with h(0)=0 but graph has infinite slope at x=0
解题入口

Use the second fundamental theorem for f'(x). For part (e), apply change of variables in the integrals. For (iii), use z = xt² and part (ii).

完成后检查

  • Verify cos(t²) is even
  • Check critical points satisfy f'(x)=cos(x²)=0
  • Verify change of variables algebraically for each part
