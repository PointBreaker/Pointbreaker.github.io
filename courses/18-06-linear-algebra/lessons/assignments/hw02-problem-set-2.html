Problem 2.5.24 Inverse of an upper triangular matrix via Gauss-Jordan Gauss-Jordan elimination · Upper triangular inverse

Use Gauss-Jordan elimination on [U I] to find the upper triangular U^−1, where U = [[1, a, b], [0, 1, c], [0, 0, 1]] (so U U^−1 = I).

  1. Row-reduce [U | I] to [I | U^−1].
  2. Write U^−1 explicitly.

公式与已知条件

  • U = [[1, a, b], [0, 1, c], [0, 0, 1]]
解题入口

Clear above each pivot starting from the bottom row, tracking the row operations on the augmented identity.

完成后检查

  • Multiply your U^−1 by U and confirm the result is I.

Problem 2.5.40 Inverse of a 4x4 bidiagonal matrix Bidiagonal matrix · Inverse via series · Gauss-Jordan

(Recommended) A is a 4 by 4 matrix with 1's on the diagonal and −a, −b, −c on the superdiagonal. Find A^−1 for this bidiagonal matrix.

  1. Find A^−1, either by row reduction or by a series expansion.

公式与已知条件

  • A = I − N, with N having a,b,c on the superdiagonal and zeros elsewhere
  • N^4 = 0
解题入口

Row-reduce [A | I], or write A = I − N and use (I−N)^−1 = I + N + N^2 + N^3 since N^4=0.

完成后检查

  • Multiply A by your proposed A^−1 and confirm the result is I.

Problem 2.6.13 LU of a symmetric matrix LU factorization · Symmetric matrices · Pivot conditions

(Recommended) Compute L and U for the symmetric matrix A = [[a,a,a,a],[a,b,b,b],[a,b,c,c],[a,b,c,d]]. Find four conditions on a, b, c, d to get A = LU with four pivots.

  1. Compute L and U.
  2. State the four pivot conditions.

公式与已知条件

  • A = [[a,a,a,a],[a,b,b,b],[a,b,c,c],[a,b,c,d]]
解题入口

Perform elimination step by step; because of the structure, all multipliers equal 1. The pivots are the diagonal entries of U.

完成后检查

  • Multiply L and U to recover A; verify the four diagonal entries of U are nonzero under your conditions.

Problem 2.6.18 Uniqueness of LDU factorization LDU factorization · Uniqueness · Triangular matrices

If A = LDU and also A = L1 D1 U1 with all factors invertible, then L = L1, D = D1, and U = U1. Derive the equation L1^−1 L D = D1 U1 U1^−1. Are the two sides triangular or diagonal? Deduce L = L1 and U = U1 (they all have diagonal 1's). Then D = D1.

  1. Derive L1^−1 L D = D1 U1 U1^−1.
  2. Identify the triangular/diagonal nature of each side.
  3. Deduce L=L1, U=U1, and D=D1.

公式与已知条件

  • A = LDU = L1 D1 U1; L,L1 unit lower triangular; U,U1 unit upper triangular; D,D1 diagonal.
解题入口

Cancel UU1^−1 and L1^−1 L using inverses, then argue that a matrix both lower and upper triangular must be diagonal.

完成后检查

  • Confirm the diagonal matrix has 1's on the diagonal from the unit-triangular factors, forcing it to be I.

Problem 2.6.25 LU of the 6x6 second-difference matrix K LU factorization · Second-difference matrix · Inverse of unit lower triangular

For the 6 by 6 second-difference constant-diagonal matrix K (the −1, 2, −1 matrix, toeplitz([2 -1 0 0 0 0])), put the pivots and multipliers into K = LU. (L and U have only two nonzero diagonals.) Find a formula for the (i,j) entry of L^−1.

  1. Find L and U of K.
  2. Find a formula for (L^−1)_ij.

公式与已知条件

  • K = toeplitz([2, -1, 0, 0, 0, 0]) (6x6)
解题入口

Run elimination and observe the pattern in the multipliers; compute L^−1 and look for a simple ratio pattern in entries.

完成后检查

  • Verify K = LU and test your formula against computed entries of L^−1.

Problem 2.6.26 Pattern in 7 K^−1 for the 6x6 K Inverse of second-difference matrix · Pattern recognition

If you print K^−1 it doesn't look good, but 7 K^−1 (for 6 by 6 K) looks wonderful. Write down 7 K^−1 by hand following the pattern: (1) Row 1 and column 1 are (6,5,4,3,2,1). (2) On and above the main diagonal, row i is i times row 1. (3) On and below the main diagonal, column j is j times column 1. Multiply K times that 7 K^−1 to produce 7I. (Illustrated for n=3.)

  1. Write down 7K^−1 for n=6.
  2. Verify K * (7K^−1) = 7I.

公式与已知条件

  • K = toeplitz([2,-1,0,0,0,0]) (6x6)
  • Pattern: row 1 = col 1 = (6,5,4,3,2,1); row i = i*row1 on/above diagonal; col j = j*col1 on/below diagonal
解题入口

Fill the matrix using the three rules, then multiply by K to check the 7I result.

完成后检查

  • Confirm K*(7K^−1) yields 7 on the diagonal and 0 elsewhere.

Problem 2.7.13 Permutation matrices with P^3 = I and P~/= I Permutation matrices · Order of a permutation

(a) Find a 3 by 3 permutation matrix with P^3 = I (but not P = I). (b) Find a 4 by 4 permutation P~ with P~^4 ≠ I.

  1. (a) Give a 3x3 permutation P with order 3.
  2. (b) Give a 4x4 permutation P~ whose 4th power is not I.

公式与已知条件

  • P^3 = I, P ≠ I for 3x3 case.
  • P~^4 ≠ I for 4x4 case.
解题入口

For (a) use a 3-cycle. For (b) build a block-diagonal permutation whose order does not divide 4.

完成后检查

  • Compute the relevant powers to confirm the order properties.

Problem 2.7.36 Which matrix sets are groups? Matrix groups · Closure under product and inverse

A group of matrices includes AB and A^−1 if it includes A and B. Which of these sets are groups? Lower triangular matrices L with 1's on the diagonal, symmetric matrices S, positive matrices M+, diagonal invertible matrices D, permutation matrices P, matrices with Q^T = Q^−1. Invent two more matrix groups.

  1. Determine group vs non-group for each listed set.
  2. Invent two additional matrix groups.

公式与已知条件

  • Sets: unit lower triangular L, symmetric S, positive M+, invertible diagonal D, permutations P, orthogonal-like Q with Q^T=Q^−1.
解题入口

Check closure under multiplication and inverses; find counterexamples for the non-groups.

完成后检查

  • For each non-group, exhibit a specific product or inverse that leaves the set.

Problem 2.7.40 Orthogonal matrix columns Orthogonal matrices · Column orthonormality · Rotation matrix

Suppose Q^T = Q^−1 (so Q^T Q = I). (a) Show that the columns q1,...,qn are unit vectors: ||qi||^2 = 1. (b) Show that every two distinct columns are perpendicular: qi^T qj = 0 for i ≠ j. (c) Find a 2 by 2 example with first entry q11 = cos θ.

  1. (a) Prove columns are unit vectors.
  2. (b) Prove distinct columns are orthogonal.
  3. (c) Give a 2x2 example with q11 = cos θ.

公式与已知条件

  • Q^T Q = I
  • (Q^T Q)_ij = qi^T qj
解题入口

Read off the diagonal and off-diagonal entries of Q^T Q = I. For (c), use the 2D rotation matrix.

完成后检查

  • For (c), verify Q^T Q = I with your example.

Problem 3.1.18 Subspaces of symmetric and skew-symmetric matrices Subspaces · Symmetric matrices · Skew-symmetric matrices

True or false (check addition or give a counterexample): (a) The symmetric matrices in M (with A^T = A) form a subspace. (b) The skew-symmetric matrices in M (with A^T = −A) form a subspace. (c) The unsymmetric matrices in M (with A^T ≠ A) form a subspace.

  1. (a) Symmetric matrices: subspace or not.
  2. (b) Skew-symmetric matrices: subspace or not.
  3. (c) Unsymmetric matrices: subspace or not.

公式与已知条件

  • Symmetric: A^T = A; skew-symmetric: A^T = −A; unsymmetric: A^T ≠ A.
解题入口

Check closure under addition and scalar multiplication; for false cases, find two unsymmetric matrices whose sum is symmetric.

完成后检查

  • For (c), the sum of two unsymmetric matrices can be symmetric, violating closure.

Problem 3.1.23 Column space growth when adding a column Column space · Solvability · Linear combinations

(Recommended) If we add an extra column b to a matrix A, then the column space gets larger unless ___. Give an example where the column space gets larger and an example where it doesn't. Why is Ax = b solvable exactly when the column space doesn't get larger—it is the same for A and [A b]?

  1. Fill in the blank.
  2. Give an example where column space grows and one where it doesn't.
  3. Explain the connection to solvability of Ax = b.

公式与已知条件

  • Augmented matrix [A b].
解题入口

The column space grows unless b is already in the column space of A. Connect solvability to b being a linear combination of A's columns.

完成后检查

  • Verify your examples: in one case b is not in col(A), in the other it is.

Problem 3.1.30 Sum of two subspaces Subspaces · Sum of subspaces · Span

Suppose S and T are two subspaces of a vector space V. (a) Definition: The sum S+T contains all sums s+t with s in S and t in T. Show that S+T satisfies the requirements (addition and scalar multiplication) for a vector space. (b) If S and T are lines in R^m, what is the difference between S+T and S∪T? Explain: The span of S∪T is S+T.

  1. (a) Show S+T is a vector space.
  2. (b) Compare S+T vs S∪T for lines; explain the span statement.

公式与已知条件

  • S+T = {s+t : s∈S, t∈T}
  • S∪T = union of S and T
解题入口

For (a), check closure directly. For (b), note S∪T is generally not closed under addition, whereas S+T is; the span of the union is the smallest subspace containing both.

完成后检查

  • For (b), if S and T are distinct lines, S+T should be a plane and S∪T should not be a subspace.

Problem 3.1.32 Column space of A vs [A AB] Column space · Matrix powers · Invertibility

Show that the matrices A and [A AB] (with extra columns) have the same column space. But find a square matrix with C(A^2) smaller than C(A). Important point: An n by n matrix has C(A) = R^n exactly when A is an ___ matrix.

  1. Show col(A) = col([A AB]).
  2. Find a square A with col(A^2) strictly smaller than col(A).
  3. Fill in: C(A)=R^n exactly when A is ___.

公式与已知条件

  • Each column of AB is a combination of columns of A.
  • [A AB] is A augmented with AB.
解题入口

For the first part, use that columns of AB are linear combinations of columns of A. For the second, try a nilpotent matrix.

完成后检查

  • Verify your square A satisfies col(A^2) ⊊ col(A) by comparing dimensions.
