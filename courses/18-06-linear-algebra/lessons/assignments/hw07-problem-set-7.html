Problem 5.2.16 Fibonacci determinants from a tridiagonal matrix Determinants · Cofactors · Tridiagonal matrices · Fibonacci numbers · Recurrence relations

Let F_n be the determinant of the n×n tridiagonal matrix with 1 on the diagonal and -1 on the off-diagonals. Expand in cofactors to show that F_n = F_{n-1} + F_{n-2}. These determinants are Fibonacci numbers 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, .... (The sequence usually starts 1, 1, 2, 3 with two 1's, so our F_n is the usual F_{n+1}.)

公式与已知条件

  • F_2 = det[[1,-1],[1,1]] = 2
  • F_3 = det[[1,-1,0],[1,1,-1],[0,1,1]] = 3
  • Recurrence: F_n = F_{n-1} + F_{n-2}
解题入口

Expand the n×n determinant along the first row. The (1,1) cofactor equals F_{n-1}. The (1,2) cofactor has a 1 in column 1 with cofactor F_{n-2}; multiply by (-1)^(1+2) and by the -1 entry from position (1,2) to get +F_{n-2}.

完成后检查

  • Verify base cases F_2 = 2 and F_3 = 3
  • Confirm F_n equals the usual Fibonacci F_{n+1}

Problem 5.2.32 Second Fibonacci numbers from 1,3,1 matrix recursion Determinants · Recurrence relations · Fibonacci numbers · Cofactors

Cofactors of the 1, 3, 1 matrices in Problem 21 give a recursion S_n = 3 S_{n-1} - S_{n-2}. Amazingly that recursion produces every second Fibonacci number. Show that S_n is the Fibonacci number F_{2n+2} by proving F_{2n+2} = 3 F_{2n} - F_{2n-2}. Keep using Fibonacci's rule F_k = F_{k-1} + F_{k-2} starting with k = 2n+2.

公式与已知条件

  • Recursion: S_n = 3 S_{n-1} - S_{n-2}
  • Identity to prove: F_{2n+2} = 3 F_{2n} - F_{2n-2}
  • Fibonacci rule: F_k = F_{k-1} + F_{k-2}
解题入口

Repeatedly substitute F_{k} = F_{k-1} + F_{k-2} starting at k = 2n+2 to rewrite F_{2n+2} in terms of F_{2n} and F_{2n-2}.

完成后检查

  • Confirm the identity holds for a small n (e.g., n=1)
  • Verify the derived identity matches 3F_{2n} - F_{2n-2}

Problem 5.2.33 Symmetric Pascal matrix determinant Determinants · Pascal matrix · Cofactors · Determinant rules

The symmetric Pascal matrices have determinant 1. If I subtract 1 from the n,n entry, why does the determinant become zero? (Use rule 3 or cofactors.) The 4×4 case is shown: det[[1,1,1,1],[1,2,3,4],[1,3,6,10],[1,4,10,20]] = 1 (known) and det[[1,1,1,1],[1,2,3,4],[1,3,6,10],[1,4,10,19]] = 0 (to explain).

公式与已知条件

  • 4×4 Pascal matrix has det = 1
  • Changing entry (4,4) from 20 to 19 yields det = 0
解题入口

Changing a single entry changes the determinant by the amount of the change times the cofactor of that entry. The cofactor of the (n,n) entry is the determinant of the (n-1)×(n-1) Pascal matrix, which is also 1.

完成后检查

  • Confirm the cofactor of the (n,n) entry equals 1
  • Verify the determinant drops by exactly 1

Problem 5.3.8 Cofactors and A C^T Cofactors · Adjugate matrix · Determinants

Find the cofactors of A and multiply A C^T to find det A, where A = [[1,1,4],[1,2,2],[1,2,5]] and C = [[6,-3,0],[·,·,·],[·,·,·]]. If you change that 4 to 100, why is det A unchanged?

公式与已知条件

  • A = [[1,1,4],[1,2,2],[1,2,5]]
  • First row of C is [6, -3, 0]
  • A C^T = (det A) I
解题入口

Compute all cofactors of A to form C, then multiply A C^T. Note that changing an entry only affects det A through that entry's cofactor; if the cofactor is 0, the determinant does not change.

完成后检查

  • Verify A C^T is a scalar multiple of the identity
  • Confirm the (1,3) cofactor of A is 0

Problem 5.3.28 Jacobian of spherical coordinates Jacobian · Spherical coordinates · Partial derivatives · Volume element

Spherical coordinates ρ, θ, φ satisfy x = ρ sin θ cos φ, y = ρ sin θ sin φ, z = ρ cos θ. Find the 3×3 matrix of partial derivatives (row 1: ∂x/∂ρ, ∂x/∂θ, ∂x/∂φ). Simplify its determinant to J = ρ^2 sin θ. Then dV in spherical coordinates is ρ^2 sin θ dρ dθ dφ, the volume of an infinitesimal coordinate box.

公式与已知条件

  • x = ρ sin θ cos φ
  • y = ρ sin θ sin φ
  • z = ρ cos θ
  • J = ρ^2 sin θ
解题入口

Form the matrix of partials of (x,y,z) with respect to (ρ,θ,φ) and expand the determinant, using cos^2 + sin^2 = 1 to simplify.

完成后检查

  • Verify the bottom-right entry is 0
  • Confirm simplification yields ρ^2 sin θ

Problem 5.3.40 Jacobi formula via Laplace expansion Determinants · Laplace expansion · Jacobi formula · Tridiagonal matrices

Suppose A is a 5×5 matrix. Its entries in row 1 multiply determinants (cofactors) in rows 2–5 to give the determinant. Guess a formula for det A using 2×2 determinants from rows 1–2 times 3×3 determinants from rows 3–5. Test your formula on the -1, 2, -1 tridiagonal matrix that has determinant 6.

公式与已知条件

  • A is 5×5
  • Test matrix is the -1, 2, -1 tridiagonal matrix with det = 6
  • Formula: sum over i<j of (-1)^(i+j+1) × (2×2 det from rows 1-2, cols i,j) × (3×3 det from rows 3-5, complementary cols)
解题入口

Sum over all column pairs i<j of (-1)^(i+j+1) times the 2×2 determinant from rows 1-2 and columns i,j times the 3×3 determinant from rows 3-5 and the complementary columns (this is more commonly named after Laplace than Jacobi).

完成后检查

  • Confirm there are C(5,2) = 10 terms, but only some are nonzero for the tridiagonal case
  • Verify the formula yields 6 for the test matrix

Problem 5.3.41 Cauchy-Binet formula for det(AB) Cauchy-Binet formula · Determinants · Matrix multiplication

The 2×2 matrix AB = (2×3)(3×2) has a Cauchy-Binet formula for det(AB): det AB = sum of (2×2 determinants in A)(2×2 determinants in B). (a) Guess which 2×2 determinants to use from A and B. (b) Test your formula when the rows of A are 1,2,3 and 1,4,7 with B = A^T.

  1. (a) Guess which 2×2 determinants to use from A and B.
  2. (b) Test your formula when the rows of A are 1,2,3 and 1,4,7 with B = A^T.

公式与已知条件

  • A = [[1,2,3],[1,4,7]]
  • B = A^T
  • Formula: sum over i<j of (2×2 det from cols i,j of A) × (2×2 det from rows i,j of B)
解题入口

Sum over all pairs i<j of the product of the 2×2 determinant from columns i,j of A and the 2×2 determinant from rows i,j of B.

完成后检查

  • Verify det(A A^T) matches the sum of products
  • Confirm the three terms sum to 24

Problem 6.1.19 Information from eigenvalues 0, 1, 2 Eigenvalues · Rank · Determinants · Matrix functions

A 3×3 matrix B is known to have eigenvalues 0, 1, 2. This information is enough to find three of these (give the answers where possible): (a) the rank of B, (b) the determinant of B^T B, (c) the eigenvalues of B^T B, (d) the eigenvalues of (B^2 + I)^{-1}.

  1. (a) the rank of B
  2. (b) the determinant of B^T B
  3. (c) the eigenvalues of B^T B
  4. (d) the eigenvalues of (B^2 + I)^{-1}

公式与已知条件

  • Eigenvalues of B: 0, 1, 2
  • det(B) = 0·1·2 = 0
  • If Ax = λx then p(A)x = p(λ)x
解题入口

(a) Use that rank equals the number of nonzero eigenvalues only when diagonalizable; argue via column space. (b) det(B^T B) = (det B)^2. (c) Note B^T B eigenvalues are not determined by B's eigenvalues alone. (d) Apply the function p(t) = 1/(t^2+1) to each eigenvalue.

完成后检查

  • Confirm rank is 2
  • Confirm det(B^T B) = 0
  • Confirm eigenvalues of (B^2+I)^{-1} are 1, 1/2, 1/5

Problem 6.1.29 Eigenvalues of triangular and patterned matrices Eigenvalues · Triangular matrices · Characteristic polynomial · Projection matrices

(Review) Find the eigenvalues of A, B, and C: A = [[1,2,3],[0,4,5],[0,0,6]] and B = [[0,0,1],[0,2,0],[3,0,0]] and C = [[2,2,2],[2,2,2],[2,2,2]].

公式与已知条件

  • A is upper triangular
  • B has characteristic polynomial (2-λ)(λ^2-3)
  • C = 6 × projection onto (1,1,1)
解题入口

For triangular A, read eigenvalues off the diagonal. For B, compute det(B - λI). For C, note it is a multiple of a rank-1 projection.

完成后检查

  • Confirm eigenvalues of A are 1, 4, 6
  • Confirm eigenvalues of B are 2, ±√3
  • Confirm eigenvalues of C are 6, 0, 0

Problem 6.2.37 Left eigenvectors and rank-1 decomposition Left eigenvectors · Rank-1 decomposition · Diagonalization · Transpose

The transpose of A = S Λ S^{-1} is A^T = (S^{-1})^T Λ S^T. The eigenvectors in A^T y = λy are the columns of (S^{-1})^T. They are often called left eigenvectors. How do you multiply matrices to find this formula for A? A = sum of rank-1 matrices: A = λ_1 x_1 y_1^T + ... + λ_n x_n y_n^T.

公式与已知条件

  • A = S Λ S^{-1}
  • A^T = (S^{-1})^T Λ S^T
  • A = Σ λ_i x_i y_i^T
解题入口

Take the transpose of A = S Λ S^{-1} and compare with the diagonalization form A^T = S_{new} Λ S_{new}^{-1} to identify the eigenvector matrix (S^{-1})^T. The rank-1 decomposition comes from columns of S times rows of Λ S^{-1}.

完成后检查

  • Confirm (S^{-1})^T diagonalizes A^T
  • Verify the rank-1 sum reconstructs A

Problem Challenge Toeplitz determinants Toeplitz matrices · Determinants · Cofactors · Recurrence relations · MATLAB

In MATLAB (and GNU Octave), the command A=toepliz(v) produces a symmetric matrix with constant descending diagonals and first row v. More generally, let v(n) be the vector in R^n with a 1 in the second and last places and 0s elsewhere, and let A(n) = toepliz(v(n)). (a) Experiment with n = 5,...,12 in MATLAB to see the repeating pattern of det A(n). (b) Expand det A(n) in terms of cofactors of the first row and in terms of cofactors of the first column. Use the known determinant C_n of problem 5.2.13 to recover the pattern found in part (a).

  1. (a) Experiment with n = 5,...,12 in MATLAB to see the repeating pattern of det A(n).
  2. (b) Expand det A(n) using cofactors of the first row and first column. Use the known determinant C_n of problem 5.2.13 to recover the pattern.

公式与已知条件

  • v(n) has 1 in positions 2 and n, 0 elsewhere
  • A(n) = toepliz(v(n))
  • Pattern for n=5..12: 2, -4, 2, 0, 2, -4, 2, 0
  • C_n from problem 5.2.13
  • det A(n) = 2(C_n - (-1)^n)
解题入口

Expand along the first row and then down both first columns to relate det A(n) to C_{n-2} and sign terms. Use the known formula for C_n to simplify.

完成后检查

  • Verify the pattern 2, -4, 2, 0 repeats
  • Confirm det A(n) = 2(C_n - (-1)^n) reproduces the pattern
